A little world,
shaped by your journey

Gitakure is a peaceful low-poly countryside that runs in your browser. There is nothing to complete. Explore a village, sit by the water, watch the monsoon come in, and stay by the fire as long as you like.

01The near bank

Most games ask for your attention.
This one gives it back.

  1. Rest is the mechanic There are no energy meters, no streaks, no crops that die. Sitting down is a real activity with its own animation, camera and sound.
  2. Nothing is missable The valley does not decay while you are away and nothing expires. A quiet week is not a failure state.
  3. Atmosphere over objectives Weather, light and sound carry the experience. There is no quest log, no XP, no ranking.

The valley is a real 3D scene, not a stack of sprites. Terrain is generated from a seeded heightfield; the river is a shader; trees, houses and props are built from geometry at runtime.

It runs on integrated graphics: pixel ratio capped at 1.5, shadows off by default, one directional light, instanced and merged geometry.

02The shrine path

Your history becomes places,
not a dashboard

Connect a GitHub username and Gitakure reads your public history, picks a few moments that actually mean something, and turns them into objects in the world. You still have to go and find them.

What connecting actually does

Reads
Public profile, public repositories, and a sample of commits from your five most recently pushed repos.
Requests
Public read scope only. Gitakure never asks for repository write access.
Stores
Keepsakes and player state in your own IndexedDB. A token, if you supply one, is used for that request and never written to storage.
Requires
Nothing. The valley is complete offline; GitHub only adds keepsakes.

03The north field

Built from geometry,
not from a modelling suite

There is no Blender in this workflow and no dedicated GPU. Every tree, house and prop is generated in TypeScript at runtime, so adding to the valley is a code change rather than an art pipeline.

Two kinds of asset

Eighty Quaternius nature models (CC0) ship with the project and are served locally as GLB. Everything else is procedural: palms, bamboo, sakura, pine, Japanese and Kerala houses, torii, bridges, rocks, grass, flowers.

A coconut palm is a curved trunk tube with drooping frond cones and optional coconuts. A bamboo cluster is segmented stalks with visible nodes. Both take a seed, so no two instances match.

The ground is real

Terrain is a seeded heightfield: rolling hills, a river channel carved along a sine curve, and a flattened plateau where the village sits. It is vertex-coloured by height and slope — sand at the waterline, grass on the flats, rock on the steep faces.

A single heightAt(x, z) sampler places every object and keeps the player on the surface. The river is a custom shader with scrolling noise and a fresnel tint.

Living, on a budget

A ten-minute day moves the sun through colour and intensity. Weather cycles clear, cloudy, rain and mist, driving a particle column and the fog density. Five seasons rotate on their own clock.

Audio is synthesised in the browser — filtered noise for wind and water, chirps for birds and crickets. There are no audio files to download.

Two cameras, one key

The default is an elevated third-person follow with damping and obstruction handling. Press V and it becomes first-person. Follow distance, field of view and camera mode are all in settings and persist between sessions.

Movement is camera-relative with acceleration and deceleration, and the character is procedural too — built from primitives, animated in code.

There is a workshop, and it is not for players

A separate dev-only page renders any procedural asset with live sliders for height, thickness, colour, counts and seed, then exports it as GLB. It is a separate build entry, so it never ships in the player bundle.

04The gate

Run it locally

Requires Bun 1.4 or newer. There is no asset build step and no setup beyond installing.

# install and run
bun install
bun run dev

Serves on localhost:5173
